Where Canada's jobs are going

For two years the unemployment rate barely moved while the composition of Canadian work was rewritten underneath it. That quiet phase has ended.

Two things turned since the summer. Employment has now fallen two months running, down 110,000 across August and September — and health care, the one sector that had grown every month since 2022, finally shrank. Meanwhile the Bank of Canada raised rates on 28 October, its first hike since 2023, because oil-driven inflation is near 3%. Policy is tightening into a weakening job market.

October data lands tomorrow

This page reflects the September survey, released 9 October. Statistics Canada publishes October figures on 6 November, which could confirm or break the two-month decline.

Monthly moves are mostly noise

The survey samples 65,000 households. Two consecutive declines are harder to dismiss than one, but six-month trends remain the real signal.

Exposure is not job loss

Exposure scores measure what AI could do to a job's tasks, not what employers have done. Roughly 90% of AI-adopting Canadian firms report no staffing change.

Trade and immigration are both unresolved

Talks on easing steel and auto tariffs resumed in late October with no deal announced, and US duties are set to rise on 1 January. The 2027–2029 immigration plan has not been tabled.
